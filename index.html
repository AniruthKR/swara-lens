How to read this

  • Swaras sit on a just-intonation grid above your Sa. Where R2 and G1 (or R3 and G2) share a position, the shared name is used (R2, G2, D2, N2).
  • Kampita is a fast oscillation (about three or more swings a second), andolita a slower swing, and a slide is a steady drift inside one note.
  • Concert recordings: the app follows the most prominent melodic line (usually the singer, or the violin or veena if it is louder) and ignores the steady drone and percussion. If it finds a second line it shows it faintly and lets you switch. It follows the louder line; it does not know which instrument is which, and a violin playing the same notes as the singer counts as one line.
  • Raga: shown with a confidence level. It reads which swaras are used and how the lines rise and fall, and it also tests other possible Sa positions, because the same notes are a different raga under a different Sa. Ragas with identical notes can only be told apart by their phrases.
  • Gamakas are found with simple pitch-movement rules, so treat them as hints, not as a transcription a guru would sign off.
  • The raga suggestion looks at which swaras are used and for how long. It does not hear characteristic phrases, so ragas with identical notes are grouped.
